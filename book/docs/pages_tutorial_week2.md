## Task 2.1a: Introductions and Recap of Preparation Week Videos (30 minutes)

As an icebreaker, please **introduce yourself** and share **one aspect from the web history videos** that impressed you or taught you something new about web history that you didn’t know before.

In Week 1 (preparation week), you watched videos on the **history and possible futures of the web**. For example, did you know that in **1989, Tim Berners-Lee proposed a networked hypertext system at CERN**, which became the **World Wide Web**? Since then, the web has evolved from the **read-only Web 1.0** to the **interactive Web 2.0**, and now **Web 3.0**, with decentralization, blockchain technology, and AI reshaping its future.

After the introductions, we will discuss **five possible scenarios for the future of the web** in small groups. Each group will address one statement and briefly share their findings after approximately **10 minutes**:

1. In the future, most online interactions will be between bots rather than humans. How might this change the way we perceive authenticity, trust, and community on the web?
2. AI-generated content (news articles, social media posts, or art) will soon outnumber human-created content. What ethical and societal challenges does this shift present?
3. The web is becoming increasingly personalized by algorithms, and different social groups flock to different tools and platforms. Is this an inevitable trade-off for convenience, or can we design a digital future where we still share ideas and spaces beyond our own “bubbles”?
4. Decentralized platforms could replace large multinational web services and give users more control over their data. Would this lead to a more democratic internet, or introduce new forms of inequality?
5. The idea behind the Metaverse is a fully immersive digital society where people live, work, and socialize without geographical or socioeconomic barriers. Would this truly create a more equitable world?
