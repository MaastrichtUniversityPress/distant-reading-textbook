## Task 2.1: Introductions and recap of preparation week videos

As an icebreaker, please introduce yourselves and mention one aspect from the web history / future videos that impressed you or taught you something that you didn’t know before. For example, did you know about Tim Berners-Lee's role in developing a networked hypertext system at CERN, which became the World Wide Web? Since then, the web has evolved from the read-only Web 1.0 to the interactive Web 2.0, with decentralization, blockchain technology, and AI reshaping its future (see Web 3.0).

After your introductions, we will discuss possible scenarios for the future of the web in small groups. Each group has 10 minutes to discuss and will then share their ideas with the entire tutorial group:

1. In the future, most online interactions will be between bots rather than humans. What web services would still be attractive to human users at all, and how could a machine take-over of these services be prevented?
2. AI-generated content (news articles, social media posts, or art) will soon outnumber human-created content. As a national government, what could you do to protect human authorship and ensure that journalists, artists, etc. can still earn a living?
3. The web is becoming increasingly personalized by algorithms, and different social groups each flock to their preferred tools and platforms. Is this an inevitable trade-off for convenience, or can we design a digital future where we still share ideas and spaces beyond our own “bubbles”?
4. Decentralized platforms like Mastodon and the Reddit-alternative Aether could replace large multinational web services and give users more control over their data. Would this lead to a more democratic internet, or introduce new forms of inequality?
5. The idea behind the Metaverse is a fully immersive digital world where people (using VR and AR technology) socialize, do business, or (co)create without geographical barriers. One platform following this vision is *Decentraland*, a 3D virtual world platform. On their website, the Decentraland Foundation explains: "Decentraland is where you hang out online. Every week you'll find movie screenings, live music, streaming parties, campfire hangouts and other community meetups — the kind of experiences where familiar faces keep showing up and newcomers always find their crowd. Founded in 2015 and publicly launched in 2020, Decentraland is a community-driven virtual space supported by the non-profit Decentraland Foundation and guided by its users through transparent governance." However, Decentraland has not become a mainstream success. What would a Metaverse platform have to offer to make you sign up?

## Task 2.3: Data visualization 1.0 – what charts are used for what type of data?

Data visualization is a powerful tool for communicating complex information in an accessible and engaging way. However, not all types of charts are equally suitable for all data sets, and data visualisations are never entirely neutral. The way in which you choose colours, add captions or labels, and highlight certain data points suggests a particular interpretation, so being aware of limitations and potential biases is important.

To make decisions about the right chart type for your data, you can use data visualisation **decision trees** such as [From Data to Viz](https://www.data-to-viz.com). A simpler and widely-shared overview of visualisation types and their purpose is offered in Andrew Abela's 2006 flowchart "Chart Suggestions: A Thought-Starter". Unfortunately, the original blog post is no longer online, but you can still find the overview here: [https://flowingdata.com/2009/01/15/flow-chart-shows-you-what-chart-to-use/](https://flowingdata.com/2009/01/15/flow-chart-shows-you-what-chart-to-use/)

Several of the chart types that you can find in the decision trees for data visualisation also matter in computational text analysis and are used in Voyant Tools. Depending on how much time is left in the tutorial session, you can now get together in small groups of 3 or 4 students and discuss the screenshots from the Voyant suite below:

1) What types of charts do you see here?
2) Do the charts show comparisons, compositions, relationships or distributions (according to Abela's flowchart)?
3) How many variables or data points do you see?
4) What data labels / numerical scales are used and why?

During class and / or after class, write down your observations and bring them to the next tutorial session as we will discuss them in the first part of the meeting.
