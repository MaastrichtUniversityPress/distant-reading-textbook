## Lecture: Decentring Old Hierarchies - Postcolonialism, Decolonialism, and New Media
### Lecturer: Monika Barget

This lecture introduces you to the history of postcolonial thought and the different (sometimes conflicting) theoretical and thematic approaches that researchers from around the world develop under this umbrella. Broadly speaking, postcolonial theory is a multi-disciplinary approach in both the humanities and social sciences that focuses on the cultural, political and economic legacy of colonialism and imperialism, particularly, but not exclusively, on European imperial power. While it began as an area of academic study in the 1970s, postcolonial novels date back almost a century earlier (for example, Joseph Conrad’s Heart of Darkness). Decolonial theory, on the other hand, is a much newer concept that focuses on untangling the production of knowledge from a western American and Euro-centric perspective. As such, it questions the perceived superiority of white, western knowledge and knowledge production. Naturally, postcolonial and decolonial theory also confront power relations and cultures of representation in the digital sphere, e.g. on social media. In this lecture, you will learn how postcolonial and decolonial frameworks can inspire your media studies research, but we will also critically reflect on the controversies within the field.
