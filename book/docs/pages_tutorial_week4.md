### Task 4.1: Homework Discussion – Participatory Culture

Participatory culture refers to the **ways in which audiences actively engage with, contribute to, and shape media and cultural content**. In this session, we will discuss the **concept of participatory culture** and its implications for media studies, focusing on how digital platforms enable users to create, share, and remix content.

We will explore examples of participatory culture, such as **fan communities, user-generated videos, and collaborative projects**, and discuss how these phenomena challenge traditional notions of authorship and authority. For instance, how do fans reinterpret or expand on existing media narratives? How do platforms like YouTube or TikTok facilitate creative expression and community-building?

This session will also consider the **ethical and practical challenges** of studying participatory culture, such as issues of **copyright, privacy, and representation**. By the end of the discussion, you will have a deeper understanding of how participatory culture shapes media and society, and how to analyze it in your own research.


### Task 4.2: Discussing an MA Thesis Using Distant Reading with Voyant

In this session, we will examine **Nicole Schanzmeyer’s MA thesis**, which uses **distant reading and Voyant Tools** to analyze reviews of true crime podcasts. This case study demonstrates how **computational text analysis** can uncover patterns in audience reception, cultural themes, and societal debates.

We will discuss the **methodology and findings** of Schanzmeyer’s research, focusing on how she used Voyant Tools to **identify recurring themes, sentiments, and keywords** in podcast reviews. How did she use distant reading to **contextualize her findings** within broader discussions of gender, race, and media ethics?

This session will also provide an opportunity to **reflect on the strengths and limitations** of using distant reading for qualitative research. By the end of the discussion, you will have a better understanding of how to **apply computational methods** to your own projects and how to interpret the results critically.
