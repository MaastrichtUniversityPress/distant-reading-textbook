### Task 4.1: Homework Discussion – Participatory Culture

Participatory culture refers to the **ways in which audiences actively engage with, contribute to, and shape media and cultural content**. In this session, we will discuss the **concept of participatory culture** and its implications for media studies, focusing on how digital platforms enable users to create, share, and remix content.

The Web's evolution allows users to contribute to political, social and cultural debates. Jenkins et al. (2013) argue that the media landscape is changing due to a shift in the relationship between the creators and consumers of content. The public is no longer satisfied to be merely consumers of content but actively participate in the production of it. Users can now shape and share information in ways that couldn’t be conceived before. Consider, for example, the recent hashtag activism movements (Clark-Parsons, 2021) #metoo, #FreeBritney, and #AshaAmini. What role do such participatory practices play in political discussion and mobilisation, and how do social media affordances enable collective identity construction (Khazraee & Novak, 2018)? However, scholars such as Christian Fuchs (2014), consider Jenkins’ approach too optimistic and superficial. The fact that a participatory web gives more people the possibility of spreading their ideas is not necessarily beneficial to democratic societies. Scholars such as Porlezza (2019), have also been noticing that due to phenomena such as trolls, incivility, and hate speech, participation fatigue has emerged. Similarly, mainstream social media platforms have gradually started being replaced by alternative, decentralised platforms (Mannell and Smith 2022). But how do they enact the notions of participatory culture? And, consequently, do we need to reconceptualise participation in the future of social media?

### Task 4.2: Discussing an MA Thesis Using Distant Reading with Voyant

In this session, we will examine **Nicole Schanzmeyer’s MA thesis**, which uses **distant reading and Voyant Tools**. This thesis, written in 2022/2023, offered "a critical look into audiences of true crime podcasts" (available internally for UM members here) and explored true crime podcast reviews mainly through the lens of cultivation theory. For this purpose, Nicole collected both podcast reviews and Reddit posts to learn about listener perceptions. However, the topic can also be analyzed through feminist theory or a focus on the public sphere, two frameworks that we teach in Maastricht. The genre can both reinforce and challenge traditional gender roles, depending on how narratives are constructed and received. (Linke & Brune, 2025) Moreover, women may see engaging with crime as empowering in a world where they are often victims.

From the perspective of public sphere theory, true crime may function as a sphere where audiences collectively engage in debates about justice, ethics, and institutional accountability. Podcasts, in particular, create spaces for audiences to react and critique.

As the data set that Nicole collected on several highly popular true crime podcasts was too large to meaningfully code by hand, she used distant reading with Voyant Tools as the primary method. To create sub-corpora, she often clustered the podcast reviews by star-ratings, which allowed her to directly compare negative with positive perceptions.
