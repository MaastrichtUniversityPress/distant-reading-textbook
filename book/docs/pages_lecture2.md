# Lecture: Feminist Theory and the World of (Big) Data

**Lecturer:** Monika Barget

---

## Description

The first part of this lecture will offer an **introduction to the history of feminism and gender studies**, with a specific focus on the emergence of **data feminism**. Data feminism asserts that data are **not neutral or objective**, but products of (potentially biased and unequal) social conditions. Countering the idea that the web is a democratic, free space that automatically fosters participation (cf. early cyber-feminism), the lecture will discuss cases in which **accessibility of data alone does not suffice** to understand the history of women and other minorities.

Applying an **intersectional approach**, the lecture will show that factors like **sex and gender** often overlap with questions of **class, ethnicity, physical ability, or religion**, and that the emancipation of outstanding, privileged individuals can deflect our attention from other members of the same community.

The second part of the lecture will offer the opportunity to **actively engage with feminist theory and data analysis**. For this purpose, we will use reviews of the **BBC podcast *28ish Days Later***. This podcast is about the female reproductive cycle—a natural yet often taboo topic in many contexts.

The readings below are **not obligatory** but can serve as a starting point if you want to work with feminist theory in your final essays.
