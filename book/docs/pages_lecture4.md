## Lecture: Understanding the Public Sphere(s)
### Lecturer: Costas Papadopoulos

This lecture will introduce the idea of the public sphere and how it relates to (digital) media. A good public sphere is seen as the elixir of modern democracy, providing a forum for exchanging views and formulating opinions (Habermas, 1962; Fraser, 1990). Media plays an important role in structural transformations of the public sphere. The printing press, for example, was very influential in the emergence of the modern public sphere. Different media allow individuals to engage with content and their social environments in distinct ways. But how have digital media transformed the very structure of the public sphere, and how does technology intervene to create new narratives and as a medium for public debate? Digital media and platforms like social networking sites can function as virtual spaces where people deliberate about different issues. Habermas described the public sphere not as a ‘thing’ but as a matter of interaction. Such interaction is now largely digitally mediated. Some scholars argue that digital technologies may open up new civic engagement and participation opportunities. They have likewise examined how emerging ‘new public spheres’ may affect established public spheres − potentially in detrimental ways. Considering the broad range of new public spheres and social interaction in these contexts, it is still under investigation if Habermas’ original concept is still applicable today. Notions such as private spheres, agonistic public spheres, and anti-public spheres have emerged as a response to questions of the relevance of public spheres today.
