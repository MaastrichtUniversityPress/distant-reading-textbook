### Task 5.1: Homework Discussion – Distant Reading in Practice

In this session, we will discuss experiences with **applying distant reading techniques** to your own research or case studies. Distant reading, as introduced by Franco Moretti, involves **analyzing large corpora of text to identify patterns, themes, and structures** that might not be visible through close reading alone.

We will explore how distant reading can be used to **study cultural phenomena, media narratives, and public discourse**. For example, how can you use tools like Voyant to **analyze trends in social media comments, podcast reviews, or news articles**? What insights can you gain from **word frequency analysis, co-occurrence networks, or sentiment analysis**?

This session will also address the **challenges and limitations** of distant reading, such as the need for **clean data, appropriate tools, and critical interpretation**. By the end of the discussion, you will have a clearer understanding of how to **integrate distant reading into your research workflow** and how to use it to support your arguments.


### **Task 5.2: Digital (Anti-)Public Spheres – How to Apply Theoretical Concepts to Data

In the second half of this tutorial, we will explore the concept of digital public spheres (see lecture) and consider how concepts related to this theory can be applied to current case studies from the world of social media. One important question to ask in this context how platform design, algorithmic curation, and user activities shape debates and foster or hinder democratic decision-making. Looking at the examples below, try to phrase research questions that would allow you to study the data through distant reading and with a focus on public spheres. You can work in small groups and select circa 5 case studies each. Share your final research questions with the class!

1. **#MeToo Movement**: a global movement exposing sexual harassment and assault.
2. **#TimesUp**: a follow-up to #MeToo, focusing on systemic change in industries like Hollywood.
3. **#OscarsSoWhite**: a critique of racial exclusion in the film industry.
4. **#BlackLivesMatter**: a movement addressing police brutality and systemic racism.
5. **#StopAsianHate**: a response to anti-Asian violence.
6. **Kim Kardashian’s "Bo Derek" Braids Controversy**: accusations of cultural appropriation of Black hairstyles.
7. **Kylie Jenner’s "Forbes Cover" Backlash**: criticism of her being labeled a "self-made billionaire" while exploiting Black culture.
8. **The "Karen" Meme**: a viral critique of white entitlement, but also debated for its gendered implications.
9. **#Gamergate**: exposing misogyny in gaming and other online communities.
10. **The "Incels" (Involuntary Celibates) Movement**: a male online subculture promoting violence against women.
11. **Revenge Porn and Deepfake Pornography**: weaponization of AI and social media against women.
12. **#RhodesMustFall**: movement challenging colonial legacies in education and public spaces.
13. **#LandBack**: indigenous-led movements reclaiming land and sovereignty through digital activism.
14. **#EndSARS (Nigeria)**: protest against police brutality, organized largely through social media.
15. **#Palestine on Social Media**: the role of digital platforms in shaping narratives around the Israeli-Palestinian conflict, including accusations of censorship and bias.

Each can be examined through **feminist, postcolonial, or critical race theory** frameworks --- but also from a public sphere perspective!!
